# United States v. Reynolds, 235 U.S. 133 (1914)

**Nivel de autoridad:** 4 — precedente de case law transversal. *(**Corte Suprema — vinculante para todos**; ver Nota de nivel.)*

**Por qué se referencia (proposición):** el *Appendix: Case Law References for T Visa Adjudications* (3 USCIS-PM B) lo cita bajo **un** concepto — *abuse or threatened abuse of legal process*. Línea de peonaje: *Kozminski* la caracteriza como el fallo donde "la compulsión de servicio por el temor constante a la prisión bajo las leyes penales" viola la 13ª Enmienda (487 U.S. 931, 943).

## Holding (verbatim)

> "In our opinion, this system is in violation of rights intended to be secured by the Thirteenth Amendment, as well as in violation of the statutes to which we have referred, which the Congress has enacted for the purpose of making that amendment effective. … It follows that the judgment of the District Court must be reversed."

## Hechos (verbatim del tribunal)

> "In No. 478, one Ed Rivers, having been convicted in a court of Alabama of the offense of petit larceny, was fined $15, and costs $43.75. The defendant Reynolds appeared as surety for Rivers, and a judgment by confession was entered up against him for the amount of the fine and costs, which Reynolds afterwards paid to the State. On May 4, 1910, Rivers, the convict, entered into a written contract with Reynolds to work for him as a farm-hand for the term of nine months and twenty-four days … under threats of arrest and imprisonment if he ceased to perform such work and labor, he worked until the sixth day of June, when he refused to labor."

## Alcance / cómo citar

USCIS cita este caso bajo *abuse or threatened abuse of legal process* — el sistema de fianza criminal como coerción por temor a la prisión (\*146-147):

> "This labor is performed under the constant coercion and threat of another possible arrest and prosecution in case he violates the labor contract which he has made with the surety, and this form of coercion is as potent as it would have been had the law provided for the seizure and compulsory service of the convict. Compulsion of such service by the constant fear of imprisonment under the criminal laws renders the work compulsory … the convict is thus kept chained to an ever-turning wheel of servitude to discharge the obligation which he has incurred to his surety …"

> **Historia posterior — línea peonaje/13ª Enmienda, VIGENTE (verificada).** Reynolds sigue siendo **good law** (13ª Enmienda / Antipeonage Act); *Kozminski* (1988) la reafirma (\*943).
