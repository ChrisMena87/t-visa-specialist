# United States v. Djoumessi, 538 F.3d 547 (6th Cir. 2008)

**Nivel de autoridad:** 4 — precedente de corte federal de apelaciones (6th Cir.), *Published*.

**Por qué se referencia (proposición):** es un **caso multi-concepto** del *Appendix: Case Law References for T Visa Adjudications* (3 USCIS-PM B): USCIS lo cita bajo **tres** conceptos — *threats of harm or serious harm*, *abuse or threatened abuse of legal process*, y *duration of victimization*. Sostiene una condena por **involuntary servitude** (18 U.S.C. § 1584) contra empleadores que retuvieron a una menor de 14 años traída de Camerún, mediante abuso físico, amenazas de cárcel/deportación por su estatus irregular, y aprovechamiento de sus vulnerabilidades.

## Holding (verbatim)

> "[T]he term 'involuntary servitude' necessarily means a condition of servitude in which the victim is forced to work for the defendant by the use or threat of physical restraint or physical injury, or by the use or threat of coercion through law or the legal process. This definition encompasses those cases in which the defendant holds the victim in servitude by placing the victim in fear of such physical restraint or injury or legal coercion. … A § 1584 conviction thus requires proof that the defendant intentionally held the victim in service against her will in one of three ways: (1) by physical restraint or force, (2) by legal coercion or (3) by threats of physical force or legal coercion. … We affirm."

*(La definición de "involuntary servitude" es del tribunal citando* United States v. Kozminski*, 487 U.S. 931, 952 (1988); es el pasaje que USCIS invoca para el concepto de threats — ver Alcance.)*

## Hechos (verbatim del tribunal)

> "In 1996, Joseph and Evelyn Djoumessi, immigrants from Cameroon living in a Detroit suburb, arranged for then-fourteen-year-old Pridine Fru to immigrate to the United States from Cameroon under a false name and with a fraudulent passport. …
>
> She worked every day from 6:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. for no compensation other than room and board, and the Djoumessis never sent her to school. … The Djoumessis also closely controlled Fru's contact with outsiders, rarely allowing her to leave the property … and telling her that if she ever contacted the police she would go to jail because she was in the country illegally. When the Djoumessis were not satisfied with Fru's work, they beat her and threatened her. And on top of all of this, Joseph Djoumessi sexually abused Fru on three occasions."

## Alcance / cómo citar

El apéndice del PM ancla este caso a **tres conceptos**; los pasajes que USCIS invoca (verbatim de la opinión):

1. **Threats of harm or serious harm** — la definición de "involuntary servitude" (en \*547, citando *Kozminski*): ver el bloque **Holding** arriba. USCIS cita a Djoumessi por reafirmar el estándar.

2. **Abuse or threatened abuse of legal process** — amenaza de cárcel/deportación atada al estatus migratorio irregular como coerción legal (\*552, pin del apéndice \*553):
   > "Djoumessi also threatened Fru with imprisonment, telling her that she would go to jail if she contacted the police because he would tell the police that she had entered the country illegally. … In view of her illegal status and in view of Djoumessi's and Evelyn's threats to send her back to Cameroon, Fru also described living and working in fear of deportation."

3. **Duration of victimization** — basta con que el trabajo haya sido involuntario durante alguna porción de la estadía (\*552-553):
   > "Even assuming there were moments during Fru's stay when she had an opportunity to escape (without risking imprisonment or worse), moreover, Djoumessi's argument still falls short because a rational trier of fact could conclude that Fru's labor was involuntary for at least some portion of her stay. And that involuntary portion would suffice to sustain the conviction."
