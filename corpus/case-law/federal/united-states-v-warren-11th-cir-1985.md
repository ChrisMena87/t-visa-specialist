# United States v. Warren, 772 F.2d 827 (11th Cir. 1985)

**Nivel de autoridad:** 4 — precedente de corte federal de apelaciones (11th Cir.), *Published*.

**Por qué se referencia (proposición):** el *Appendix: Case Law References for T Visa Adjudications* (3 USCIS-PM B) lo cita bajo **un** concepto — *threats of harm or serious harm*. Condena por **servidumbre involuntaria** (18 U.S.C. § 1584) de trabajadores migrantes reclutados con engaño y retenidos en campos de trabajo mediante un clima de miedo.

## Holding (verbatim)

> "Appellants were convicted of conspiring to hold, and actually holding, migrant workers in involuntary servitude. They contend that the evidence was insufficient to support their convictions and they ask us to acquit them. … We affirm."

*(Sobre suficiencia:)*

> "We find that, viewing the evidence in the light most favorable to the Government, there was sufficient evidence for a reasonable jury to convict appellants on all counts charged."

## Hechos (verbatim del tribunal)

> "Appellants Willie Warren, Sr., Richard Warren, Willie Warren, Jr., and Michael Moore operated agricultural labor camps which supplied field workers to farmers in Florida and North Carolina. The operation of these camps, however, involved certain irregularities concerning the recruitment and treatment of laborers. …
>
> These accounts, as well as others, revealed an operation where individuals were picked up under false pretenses, delivered to a labor camp to work long hours for little or no pay, and kept in the fields by poverty, alcohol, threats, and acts of violence."

## Alcance / cómo citar

USCIS cita una proposición bajo *threats of harm or serious harm*, en \*834 — la oportunidad de escapar no descarta la servidumbre si hay temor de daño físico:

> "Various forms of coercion may constitute a holding in involuntary servitude. The use, or threatened use, of physical force to create a climate of fear is the most grotesque example of such coercion. … That the worker had the opportunity to escape is of no moment, if the defendant has placed him in such fear of physical harm that he is afraid to leave."
