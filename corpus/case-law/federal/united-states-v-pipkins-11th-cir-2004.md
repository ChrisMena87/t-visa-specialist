# United States v. Pipkins, 378 F.3d 1281 (11th Cir. 2004)

**Nivel de autoridad:** 4 — precedente de corte federal de apelaciones (11th Cir.), *Published*.

**Por qué se referencia (proposición):** el *Appendix: Case Law References for T Visa Adjudications* (3 USCIS-PM B) lo cita bajo **un** concepto — *duration of victimization*. Condena por **servidumbre involuntaria** (18 U.S.C. § 1584) de una menor de 15 años (JF5) prostituida; el elemento "any term" del § 1584 significa que la duración puede ser breve.

## Holding (verbatim)

> "Pipkins contends that the evidence was insufficient to support his conviction for unlawfully and willfully holding or selling 15-year-old JF5 in involuntary servitude, in violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1584 … We therefore conclude that the evidence was sufficient to support Pipkins's conviction for involuntary servitude of JF5."

*(Disposición general: "We affirm the Defendants' convictions and sentences".)*

## Hechos (verbatim del tribunal)

> "The evidence at trial demonstrated that Pipkins and Moore prostituted juvenile females— at least one of whom was as young as 12 — from at least 1997 until their arrest in late 2001. …
>
> Pipkins held JF5 in involuntary servitude by forcing her to prostitute herself and turn over all of her earnings to him, lest she endure a beating at his hands."

## Alcance / cómo citar

USCIS cita una proposición bajo *duration of victimization*, en \*1297 — el "any term" del § 1584:

> "Section 1584 requires that involuntary servitude be for 'any term,' which suggests that the temporal duration can be slight. Thus, the language of § 1584 negates Pipkins's argument that JF5 was never in involuntary servitude because she freely traveled between pimps. The record supports a finding that Pipkins held JF5 in involuntary servitude for at least part of the time that she prostituted for him."
