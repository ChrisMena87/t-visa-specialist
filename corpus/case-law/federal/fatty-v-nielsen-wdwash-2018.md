# Fatty v. Nielsen, No. C17-1535-MJP (W.D. Wash. July 20, 2018)

**Nivel de autoridad:** 4 — precedente de case law transversal (**orden de distrito NO PUBLICADA**; ver Nota de nivel — es la especie más baja del Nivel 4). *(Juez: Marsha J. Pechman.)*

**Por qué se referencia (proposición):** uno de los 7 casos del README de la KB (frente **SEPARADO** del apéndice del PM). El README lo etiqueta "habeas para T applicants con removal order". La proposición citable es el **holding directo**: un tribunal de distrito tiene jurisdicción de habeas (§ 2241) para **suspender la remoción** de un solicitante de Visa T que ya tiene **orden final de remoción**, mientras USCIS adjudica la solicitud — porque la presencia física en EE. UU. es condición de elegibilidad para la Visa T y removerlo lo privaría permanentemente de una defensa viable a la remoción.

## Holding (verbatim del tribunal)

> "the Court declines to adopt the Report and Recommendation; GRANTS Petitioner's Motion to Stay Removal; DENIES Respondents' Motion to Dismiss, and DENIES Petitioner's Motion to Enforce Court Order."

De la Conclusión (\*9-10):

> "(2) Having found that due process requires that Mr. Fatty's removal be stayed pending adjudication of his T visa application, that Mr. Fatty will suffer irreparable harm absent a stay, and that the government will not be burdened by a stay, the Court GRANTS Petitioner's Motion to Stay Removal … ; (3) Having found that it has jurisdiction to hear Mr. Fatty's due process and APA claims, … the Court DENIES Respondents' Motion to Dismiss …"

## Hechos (verbatim del tribunal)

> "Petitioner Bangally Fatty filed an immigration habeas petition under 28 U.S.C. § 2241 seeking a judicial stay of his removal pending adjudication of his petition for T nonimmigrant status ('T visa'). … Mr. Fatty realistically could not have applied for a T visa until the regulations changed in January 2017. … [Mr. Fatty] had a final order of removal for more than six years."

## Alcance / cómo citar

El README lo invoca por el **habeas / stay of removal para solicitantes de Visa T con orden final**. Las cadenas verbatim que sostienen la proposición:

**Jurisdicción (§ 1252(g) no la despoja) (\*2-3):**

> "The Court finds that 8 U.S.C. § 1252(g) ('Section 1252(g)') does not divest it of jurisdiction to hear Mr. Fatty's claims. … Mr. Fatty's claims are more properly categorized as collateral legal and constitutional challenges to the process by which the government seeks to remove him. The Court finds this to be a critical distinction, and one which enables it to hear his case."

**El daño que el debido proceso previene (\*4-5):**

> "If Mr. Fatty is removed before his T visa adjudicated, he will be deprived of a viable defense to removability. … However, as physical presence in the United States is a condition of eligibility, his T visa cannot be granted once he is removed. 8 C.F.R. § 214.11(g)."

> "his removal is similarly 'Kafkaesque' in that, once removed, he will be permanently precluded from pursuing a viable defense to removal. Devitri, 289 F. Supp. 3d at 294 …"

**Matiz importante — la etiqueta comprime, y el fallo NO es monolítico:** el tribunal dividió los retos APA. Halló que la determinación de *bona fide* de USCIS fue **arbitraria y caprichosa** (prematura, hecha antes del name check del FBI y antes de que Fatty suplementara la evidencia; "at the request of DHS … in response to Mr. Fatty's request to stay his removal", \*7-8). Pero **rechazó** el reto APA contra **ICE** (la denegación del stay por ICE "was neither arbitrary nor capricious", \*8). Y concurrió con la R&R en que Fatty **no** tiene un interés de propiedad protegido en la Visa T o su adjudicación (nota 2, *Ching v. Mayorkas*). La proposición citable transversal es el **holding de habeas/stay por debido proceso**, no una victoria total del peticionario.
