# TVPA Element Matrix v1 — extracción doctrinal (purgada)

## Reglas del documento

1. **Solo extracción.** Cada bloque contiene citas literales con atribución (archivo + línea). No hay reglas operacionales. No hay condicionales (`SI Q11 + Q13 entonces…`). No hay prompts. No hay heurísticas de intake.
2. **Elementos recurrentes = frases que se repiten en las fuentes.** No son interpretación: son inventario léxico/estructural de qué aparece y dónde.
3. **Test del autor.** Para toda línea no-citada: si el autor de la fuente original NO la reconocería como fiel a lo que escribió, es interpretación. Si es valiosa pero interpretativa, se mueve a la sección final "Hipótesis preliminares del preparador". Si es prescindible, se elimina.
4. **Honestidad de fuente.** Las citas son textuales. Si una cita fue editada para limpiar formato (ej. saltos de línea, asteriscos), el bloque lo marca.
5. **Honestidad de ausencia.** Cuando un elemento legal NO tiene definición en una fuente (estatuto / regulación / PM), se declara explícitamente. Una ausencia de definición es información doctrinal verificable.
6. **Fase 2 (no aquí).** Convertir esto en reglas operacionales y preguntas de intake es trabajo posterior, después de la sesión con el abogado.

## Marco general (cita literal del estatuto que define los tres ejes)

**22 U.S.C. § 7102(11)** *(fuente: `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:177-187`)*:

> "The term 'severe forms of trafficking in persons' means—
> **(A)** sex trafficking in which a commercial sex act is induced by force, fraud, or coercion, or in which the person induced to perform such act has not attained 18 years of age; or
> **(B)** the recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, or obtaining of a person for labor or services, through the use of force, fraud, or coercion for the purpose of subjection to involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage, or slavery."

**Tabla operativa USCIS** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:141-179`, transcripción literal de la tabla HTML)*:

| Type of Trafficking | Action | Purpose | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sex Trafficking** | Recruiting; Harboring; Transporting; Provision; Soliciting; Patronizing; Obtaining (Of a person) | For the purpose of a commercial sex act | Where the commercial sex act is induced by force, fraud, or coercion (Not required when the victim is under 18 years of age) |
| **Labor Trafficking** | Recruiting; Harboring; Transporting; Provision; Obtaining (Of a person) | For the purpose of subjecting the victim to: Involuntary servitude; Peonage; Debt bondage; Slavery | Through use of force, fraud, or coercion |

**Conteo de definiciones autónomas en el canon T-Visa** (hecho verificable contra las fuentes):

- ACTOS (7 listados): solo **Harboring** tiene sección de definición propia en el PM (§B.2). Recruiting, Transporting, Providing, Obtaining, Soliciting, Patronizing aparecen en las listas estatutarias, regulatorias y de PM, pero no tienen sección/entrada de definición autónoma.
- MEDIOS (3 listados): solo **Coercion** está definido en estatuto (§ 7102(3)), regulación (8 CFR § 214.201) y PM (§B.3). Force y Fraud aparecen en la fórmula pero no tienen entrada de definición propia en ningún nivel.
- FINES (5 listados): **Involuntary Servitude**, **Debt Bondage** y **Commercial Sex Act** tienen definición en estatuto y regulación. **Peonage** tiene definición regulatoria pero no estatutaria autónoma. **Slavery** está declarada por el PM como "not defined in the TVPA or regulations".

# ACTOS

> Las "acciones prohibidas" que, ejecutadas con un medio (force/fraud/coercion) y para un fin (involuntary servitude / peonage / debt bondage / slavery / commercial sex act), configuran "severe form of trafficking in persons".

### Definición legal

**Estatuto — 22 U.S.C. § 7102(11)(B)** *(fuente: `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:184-187`)*:

> "the recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, or obtaining of a person for labor or services, through the use of force, fraud, or coercion for the purpose of subjection to involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage, or slavery."

**Estatuto — 22 U.S.C. § 7102(12)** *(fuente: `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:191-193`)*:

> "The term 'sex trafficking' means the recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, obtaining, patronizing, or soliciting of a person for the purpose of a commercial sex act."

**Regulación — 8 CFR § 214.201** *(fuente: `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:71`)*: ninguna entrada autónoma para "recruitment". El término aparece dentro de la cita de "Severe form of trafficking in persons".

**Policy Manual — 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 3** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:984`)*:

> "Used a particular action (recruitment, transportation, harboring, provision, obtaining, or in the case of sex trafficking, also patronizing or soliciting)"

### Observaciones USCIS

**Sobre el "purpose-only" path (sin labor consumada)** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:988`)*:

> "If an applicant has not performed labor or services or a commercial sex act, the applicant must establish that they were recruited, transported, harbored, provided, or obtained (or in the case of sex trafficking, patronized or solicited) for the purpose of a commercial sex act or subjection to involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage, or slavery."

**Sobre actos previos a la entrada** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:250`)*:

> "Officers may consider actions taken before the victim's arrival in the United States or before the start of the performance of labor or services that are relevant to establishing a scheme, pattern, or plan."

### Elementos recurrentes

- **Ausencia de definición propia.** Ni el estatuto, ni la regulación, ni el PM definen "recruiting" como término autónomo. Aparece exclusivamente dentro de la fórmula compuesta "recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, [obtaining/patronizing/soliciting]".
- **Posición léxica.** Es el primer verbo de la cadena, tanto en § 7102(11)(B) como en § 7102(12).
- **Cita cruzada con el apéndice de case law.** El PM remite al "Appendix: Case Law References for T Visa Adjudications" en `part-b-chapter-2-eligibility-requirements.md:235, 241, 245, 257` (verificar locator: las remisiones se hacen en discusiones de involuntary servitude, labor o servicios y no-traditional work, no específicamente para "recruiting").
- **Acto consumado no requerido.** Cita literal del PM `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:355`:
  > "In determining whether an applicant has been a victim of a severe form of trafficking, it is not necessary for the victim to actually perform the labor or commercial sex act(s) to be eligible for T nonimmigrant status. For example, a victim may be recruited through force, fraud, or coercion for the purpose of performing labor or services but be rescued or have escaped before performing any labor or services."

## Harboring

### Autoridad

| Nivel | Fuente | Cita exacta del locator |
|---|---|---|
| Estatuto TVPA | 22 U.S.C. § 7102(11)(B) y § 7102(12) | `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:184, 191` |
| Regulación | 8 CFR § 214.201 | `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:71` |
| Policy Manual | 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 2, §B.2 — "Definition of Harboring" | `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:180-194` |

### Definición legal

**Estatuto y Regulación:** mismas cadenas que en Recruiting; no contienen entrada autónoma para "harboring".

**Policy Manual — 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 2, §B.2** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:182`)* — único ACTO con sección de definición propia en el PM:

> "While the term harboring is most commonly understood to mean actively hiding or concealing a fugitive, harboring within the trafficking context refers to the series of actions a trafficker takes to exert and maintain control over a victim by substantially limiting or restricting a victim's movement or agency."

*(Definición de "agency", `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:184`)*:

> "The term agency refers to the ability to act according to one's own free will, control over one's emotional and physical states of being, and ability to exert (or attempt to exert) influence over oneself.[11]"

### Observaciones USCIS

**Sobre escape posterior** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:184`)*:

> "A victim's ability to eventually escape the trafficking does not necessarily invalidate the lack of agency the victim experienced throughout the victimization."

**Factores no exhaustivos** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:184`)*:

> "Some factors officers may evaluate to determine whether harboring occurred include, but are not limited to:
> - Isolation of the victim;
> - Limitations on the victim's ability to interact with others;
> - Restrictions on the victim's movement; and
> - Consequences of acting outside of the trafficker's orders or without the trafficker's explicit permission."

**Sobre relación preexistente** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:191`)*:

> "Harboring does not require a preexisting relationship between the victim and the trafficker. However, harboring may occur within a variety of consensual relationships, including employer-employee, parent-child, smuggler-smugglee, landlord-tenant, and marriages and other intimate partner relationships."

**Sobre duración** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:193`)*:

> "Harboring may occur for any period of time, even a brief period, but generally must endure long enough to substantially limit or restrict the victim's movement or agency."

### Elementos recurrentes

- **"control over a victim"** y **"movement or agency"** aparecen ambas como expresiones nucleares en la definición del PM (línea 60).
- **"substantially limiting or restricting"** — fórmula con adverbio "substantially" sin cuantificación numérica en el PM.
- **"agency"** se define en el PM por tres componentes literales: (a) "act according to one's own free will", (b) "control over one's emotional and physical states of being", (c) "ability to exert (or attempt to exert) influence over oneself".
- **Contraste explícito con el sentido coloquial.** El PM cita un sentido común ("actively hiding or concealing a fugitive") y declara un sentido distinto ("refers to the series of actions a trafficker takes to exert and maintain control…"). Ambas frases aparecen literales en la definición.
- **Relaciones consensuales no excluyen harboring** — lista literal del PM: empleador-empleado, padre-hijo, smuggler-smugglee, propietario-inquilino, parejas íntimas.
- **"any period of time, even a brief period"** — frase literal del PM. Acoplada al cualificador funcional "but generally must endure long enough to substantially limit or restrict".
- **Único ACTO con sección de definición propia en el PM.** Hecho verificable contra el índice del Ch.2.

> - The use or threatened use of any object or weapon to intimidate or injure; and
> - The use of threats through words or actions to instill in the victim a fear that others would kill, injure, or use force against the victim or any other person."

### Definición legal

**Estatuto — 22 U.S.C. § 7102(3)** *(fuente: `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:56-64`)*:

> "The term 'coercion' means—
> **(A)** threats of serious harm to or physical restraint against any person;
> **(B)** any scheme, plan, or pattern intended to cause a person to believe that failure to perform an act would result in serious harm to or physical restraint against any person; or
> **(C)** the abuse or threatened abuse of the legal process."

**Regulación — 8 CFR § 214.201** *(fuente: `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:33`)*:

> "*Coercion* means threats of serious harm to or physical restraint against any person; any scheme, plan, or pattern intended to cause a person to believe that failure to perform an act would result in serious harm to or physical restraint against any person; or the abuse or threatened abuse of the legal process."

**Policy Manual — 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 2, §B.3** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:197`)*:

> "The regulations define coercion as:
> - Threats of serious harm to or physical restraint against any person;
> - Any scheme, plan, or pattern intended to cause a person to believe that failure to perform an act would result in serious harm to or physical restraint against any person; or
> - Abuse or threatened abuse of the legal process.[12]"

**Definición regulatoria de "serious harm" — 8 CFR § 214.201** *(fuente: `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:69`)*:

> "*Serious harm* means any harm, whether physical or nonphysical, including psychological, financial, or reputational harm, that is sufficiently serious, under all the surrounding circumstances, to compel a reasonable person of the same background and in the same circumstances to perform or to continue performing labor or services in order to avoid incurring that harm."

**Definición regulatoria de "abuse or threatened abuse of the legal process" — 8 CFR § 214.201** *(fuente: `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:25`)*:

> "*Abuse or threatened abuse of the legal process* means the use or threatened use of a law or legal process whether administrative, civil, or criminal, in any manner or for any purpose for which the law was not designed, in order to exert pressure on another person to cause that person to take some action or refrain from taking some action."

### Observaciones USCIS

El PM dedica un capítulo a coercion subdividido en cuatro modalidades. Las citas siguientes son literales.

**Threats of Serious Harm — definición expandida en el PM** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:205`)*:

> "Serious harm includes any harm, whether physical or nonphysical, including psychological, financial, or reputational harm, that is sufficiently serious when considering the surrounding circumstances to compel a reasonable person of the same background and in the same circumstances to perform or to continue performing labor or services[13] in order to avoid experiencing that harm.[14]"

**Lista no-exhaustiva de "serious harm"** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:205`)*:

> Serious harm may include various types of harm, including, but not limited to:
> - Harm to third parties close to the victim, such as family members or children in the care of the victim;
> - Banishment or expulsion from the home of the victim or family members;
> - Starvation or restriction of food;
> - Bankruptcy of the victim or family members;
> - Refusing to send money to the victim's family;
> - Withholding pay or other compensation resulting in an inability to pay off debt or obtain necessary medical care or nutrition;
> - Subjecting victims to isolation, denial of sleep, or other punishments;
> - Threats of deportation or removal from the United States;
> - Threats to report the victim to law enforcement or immigration authorities; and
> - Reputational harm, such as threats to falsely accuse the victim of being a thief or threats to disclose engagement in commercial sex, if the victim attempts to leave.

**Sobre agregación de harms** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:219`)*:

> "One type of harm alone may constitute serious harm; however, whether the harm asserted constitutes 'serious harm' depends on the facts of the case. In cases involving multiple types of harm, each type of harm may separately meet the definition of serious harm, or when considered together may constitute serious harm."

**Sobre vulnerabilidades de la víctima** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:221`)*:

> "In determining whether the trafficker made a threat of serious harm that could reasonably be believed by the victim, USCIS considers any vulnerabilities of the victim, including but not limited to the victim's particular socioeconomic situation, physical and mental condition, age, education, cultural background, training, experience, or intelligence.[15]"

**Use or Threats of Use of Physical Restraint** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:227`)*:

> "The use or threat of use of physical restraint or physical injury, including the use of violence or threats of violence, can constitute coercion.[16] Examples of physical coercion include:
> - An actual or threatened physical act that could result in harm;
> - The use or threatened use of any object or weapon to intimidate or injure; and
> - The use of threats through words or actions to instill in the victim a fear that others would kill, injure, or use force against the victim or any other person.
> There is no requirement that the victim be physically restrained and prevented from escaping in order for USCIS to find that physical coercion occurred. In addition, sexual abuse could constitute physical coercion."

**Sobre qué constituye "threat"** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:242`)*:

> "A threat is a serious expression of an intention to inflict harm, immediately or in the future, as distinguished from idle or careless talk, exaggeration, or something said in a joking manner. For an expression to be a threat, the expression must have been made under such circumstances that a reasonable person (in the victim's circumstances) who heard, read, saw, or otherwise experienced the action would perceive it to be a serious expression of an intent to cause harm. In addition, the trafficker must have made the expression intending it to be a threat, or with the knowledge that the statement would be viewed as a threat.[17]"

**Perspectiva de evaluación** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:244`)*:

> "USCIS views threats from the viewpoint of the victim, considering the victim's individual circumstances and the totality of the circumstances surrounding the trafficker's threats."

**Scheme, Pattern, or Plan** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:248`)*:

> "Instead of direct threats or use of overt violence, traffickers may employ a scheme, plan, or pattern, 'intended to cause the person to believe that, if that person did not perform such labor or services, that person or another person would suffer serious harm or physical restraint.'[18]"

**Sobre actos previos a la víctima** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:250`)*:

> "In determining whether there is such a scheme, pattern, or plan, officers should consider the totality of the trafficker's actions and any evidence of the trafficker's intent in carrying them out. Officers may consider actions taken before the victim's arrival in the United States or before the start of the performance of labor or services that are relevant to establishing a scheme, pattern, or plan."

**Lista no-exhaustiva de indicadores de scheme/pattern/plan** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:252`)*:

> "The following non-exhaustive list includes factors that may contribute to a finding of coercion by way of a scheme, pattern, or plan:
> - Limiting access to public benefits and services;
> - Confiscation of identification, travel, or other personal documents;
> - Creating a climate of social or linguistic isolation;
> - Subjecting the victim to verbal abuse, physical abuse, or harsh living or working conditions;
> - Creating a belief that the perpetrator could be physically violent through demonstrations of physical violence against workers or others;
> - Inducing workers to take on high debt;
> - Fraudulent promises about work or housing conditions;
> - Subjecting the victim to economic detriment through tactics including, but not limited to:
>   * Underpaying or not paying what was promised to the employee;
>   * Not supplying promised work hours; or
>   * Deducting expenses from pay that are unreasonable or not as contracted;
> - Creating a belief that the employer has the power to deport, arrest, or blacklist[19] a victim through actions such as, but not limited to:
>   * Discussing the employer's relationship with law enforcement;
>   * Threatening to call law enforcement or other authorities (for example, the sheriff, police, immigration authorities or Federal Bureau of Investigation) if the employee complains about conditions or leaves the job;
>   * Threatening to ensure the employee is never able to work in the United States again;
>   * Filing a criminal complaint if the employee complains about working conditions or requests unpaid wages;
>   * Threatening to report the employee as an absconder; or
>   * Creating a belief that the employer has powerful connections to those who can impose harm on the victim or victim's family members, such as connections to political leaders, local law enforcement, or organized crime, in the United States or the victim's home country."

**Abuse or Threatened Abuse of the Legal Process** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:277`)*:

> "Coercion could also include abuse or threatened abuse of the legal process, which means the use or threatened use of a law or legal process, whether administrative, civil, or criminal, in any manner or for any purpose for which the law was not designed, in order to exert pressure on another person to cause that person to take some action or refrain from taking some action.[20]"

**Lista no-exhaustiva de abuso del proceso legal** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:279`)*:

> The following is a non-exhaustive list of the types of threats that could establish abuse or threatened abuse of the legal process:
> - Threats of imprisonment, prosecution, or imposition of criminal sanctions for failure to perform labor or services;
> - Threats to institutionalize someone in a mental health facility;
> - Threats to have immigration authorities arrest or deport workers, particularly for failing to comply with the trafficker's directives or to perpetuate workers' ongoing labor by exploiting their fears; or
> - Threats to call immigration authorities, to report a worker for absconding, or to let a person's visa expire.

**Sobre amenazas legales no-realistas** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:286`)*:

> "Legal coercion may occur even when the threatened outcome is not an actual legal possibility. In determining the purpose behind the threats, USCIS examines any threat of legal consequences in light of all of the surrounding circumstances and considers whether the threat is being used for an end different from that envisioned by the law or for a coercive purpose designed to intimidate."

### Elementos recurrentes

- **Tres modalidades estatutarias canónicas:** (A) threats of serious harm/physical restraint, (B) scheme/plan/pattern, (C) abuse of legal process. Frases idénticas en estatuto, regulación y PM.
- **Alcance de "serious harm":** la regulación y el PM lo enumeran como "physical or nonphysical, including psychological, financial, or reputational harm".
- **"reasonable person of the same background and in the same circumstances"** — frase literal del estatuto y la regulación.
- **"totality of the circumstances"** — frase literal que aparece en `part-b-chapter-2-eligibility-requirements.md:122, 128, 164` y en footnote [24] (línea 600).
- **"USCIS views threats from the viewpoint of the victim"** — cita literal del PM línea 122.
- **Listas explícitamente "non-exhaustive".** Tres listas grandes (serious harm, scheme/pattern/plan, abuse of legal process) son cada una declaradas no-exhaustivas por el PM.
- **"for any purpose for which the law was not designed"** — frase literal de la regulación (línea 53) y del PM (línea 155).
- **"Legal coercion may occur even when the threatened outcome is not an actual legal possibility"** — cita literal del PM línea 164.
- **Doble requisito en la definición de "threat":** el PM línea 120 declara dos elementos: (a) que un "reasonable person" lo perciba como serio bajo las circunstancias; (b) que el trafficker lo haya hecho con intención de threat o conocimiento de que sería percibido como tal.
- **Cross-ref a 18 U.S.C. § 1589.** El PM cita §1589 en footnotes [14] y [18] (`part-b-chapter-2-eligibility-requirements.md:579, 587`) al definir "serious harm" y "scheme/plan/pattern".

## Involuntary Servitude

### Autoridad

| Nivel | Fuente | Cita exacta del locator |

### Definición legal

**Estatuto — 22 U.S.C. § 7102(8)** *(fuente: `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:124-132`)*:

> "The term 'involuntary servitude' includes a condition of servitude induced by means of—
> **(A)** any scheme, plan, or pattern intended to cause a person to believe that, if the person did not enter into or continue in such condition, that person or another person would suffer serious harm or physical restraint; or
> **(B)** the abuse or threatened abuse of the legal process."

**Regulación — 8 CFR § 214.201** *(fuente: `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:51-55`)*:

> "*Involuntary servitude,* for the purposes of this part:
> (1) Means a condition of servitude induced by means of any scheme, plan, or pattern intended to cause a person to believe that, if the person did not enter into or continue in such condition, that person or another person would suffer serious harm or physical restraint; or a condition of servitude induced by the abuse or threatened abuse of legal process; and
> (2) Includes a condition of servitude in which the victim is forced to work for the trafficker by the use or threat of physical restraint or physical injury, or by the use or threat of coercion through the law or the legal process. This definition encompasses those cases in which the trafficker holds the victim in servitude by placing the victim in fear of such physical restraint or injury or legal coercion."

**Policy Manual — 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 2, §B.4** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:293`)*:

> "Involuntary servitude is defined as a condition of servitude induced by:
> - Means of any scheme, plan, or pattern intended to cause a person to believe that, if the person did not enter into or continue in such condition, that person or another person would suffer serious harm or physical restraint; or
> - Abuse or threatened abuse of legal process.[21]"

### Observaciones USCIS

**Sobre consentimiento inicial y coerción sobrevenida** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:298`)*:

> "Involuntary servitude may occur where a victim initially voluntarily agrees to perform a service but is later forced or coerced into performing the same service. Involuntary servitude also occurs when the victim is forced to work for the perpetrator by the use or threat of physical restraint or physical injury, or by the use or threat of coercion through the law or the legal process. This definition includes those cases in which the perpetrator holds the victim in servitude by placing the victim in fear of such physical restraint or injury or legal coercion.[22] Coercion may also be established if the perpetrator uses psychological abuse."

**Sobre contexto doméstico** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:300`)*:

> "Involuntary servitude may occur in circumstances where the victim has a preexisting relationship or arrangement with the trafficker. For example, involuntary servitude may occur in the context of a domestic relationship (including parent-child, intimate partner, or roommate relationships). Involuntary servitude can include, but is not limited to, domestic servitude and sexual exploitation. Whether the conditions occurred in a domestic context or outside of the home is not in and of itself determinative of whether involuntary servitude has occurred."

**Sobre qué NO constituye condición de servidumbre** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:302`)*:

> "Conditions of servitude are defined by compulsory service or labor against a victim's will in a manner that deprives the victim of liberty. Such conditions do not include scenarios in which:
> - The victim is free to quit or leave without incurring retribution or harm (or without fear of retribution or harm);[23]
> - The victim can exercise significant control over their working conditions;[24] or
> - The victim continued working to avoid the ordinary financial, personal, or professional consequences of quitting or leaving.[25]"

*(Footnote [24], `part-b-chapter-2-eligibility-requirements.md:600`: "Whether a victim can exercise significant control over their working conditions is assessed based on the totality of the circumstances. However, involuntary servitude is not established based solely on the fact that an employer, rather than an employee, sets the terms and conditions of employment (such as wages, hours, location, and duties or responsibilities).")*

**Sobre DV como medio de servidumbre — coexistencia y test** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:310`)*:

> "Trafficking can occur alongside intimate partner abuse, and involuntary servitude and domestic violence may coexist in some situations. In determining whether abuse or threats of abuse (including physical violence, mental abuse, emotional abuse, sexual violence, intimidation, and controlling behavior in the home) create a condition of involuntary servitude that constitutes a severe form of trafficking in persons, officers should evaluate whether the situation involves:
> - Compelled or coerced labor or services; or
> - Forced sexual activity."

**Sobre el límite entre DV y trata** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:316`)*:

> "Although domestic violence and trafficking often intersect, not all work that occurs as the result of domestic violence constitutes a condition of servitude. For example, in certain contexts, the unequal assignment of household tasks among household members may signal an abusive relationship, but it does not automatically constitute the creation of a condition of servitude."

**Sobre el propósito del agresor en contexto DV** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:318`)*:

> "Forced labor[26] compelled by domestic violence occurs where the aim of the domestic violence is to force the victim to engage in labor that creates a condition of servitude. To distinguish between domestic violence and labor trafficking resulting from domestic violence, applicants must demonstrate that the perpetrator's motivation or purpose is or was to subject the applicant to a condition of servitude."

**Sobre cómo evaluar las acciones del traficante** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:320`)*:

> "Officers may evaluate the actions the trafficker has taken to maintain the applicant in a condition of servitude, including recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, obtaining, patronization, or solicitation, to further understand the goal of the perpetrator. Where trafficking is accompanied and enforced by abuse, victims may act upon the trafficker's demands for labor and services due to fear or coercion and may feel that they do not have their own liberty or self-determination."

**Lista no-exhaustiva de circunstancias de control sobre la libertad** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:322`)*:

> "The following non-exhaustive list outlines circumstances where the trafficker may control the victim's liberty to create a condition of servitude:
> - An expectation that the victim's life fulfills the orders of the trafficker (such as a demand from the trafficker to perform domestic labor at an unreasonable level, including unreasonable working hours or constant availability to perform labor regardless of health or energy);
> - Lack of control over the victim's own wages despite laboring under the trafficker's demands; or
> - The imposition of unequal living arrangements as part of the campaign of force, fraud, and coercion (for example, unequal sleeping arrangements, living arrangements, or access to nourishment)."

**Sobre smuggling voluntario** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:330`)*:

> "Aliens may experience violence over the course of their smuggling. While not all crimes or exploitation that occur during a smuggling arrangement rise to the level of trafficking, smuggling may develop into trafficking. The existence of a voluntary smuggling arrangement does not invalidate the possibility of involuntary servitude arising within the smuggling."

**Sobre labor no tradicional / contexto familiar** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:367`)*:

> "When analyzing whether a person has been subjected to involuntary servitude or peonage, USCIS considers that the labor or services might include non-traditional types of work. Appendix: Case Law References for T Visa Adjudications [3 USCIS-PM B, Appendices Tab] lists decisions that may provide additional clarification on the concepts of non-traditional work. Compelled labor or services provided in the context of a familial or intimate partner relationship may satisfy the definition."

**Sobre labor doméstica como forced labor** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:369`)*:

> "For example, in certain contexts, domestic labor may constitute forced labor and satisfy the involuntary servitude assessment, when the actions are induced by force, fraud, or coercion and where the perpetrator had a goal of securing the forced labor or services for the purpose of subjecting the victim to a condition of servitude."

**Sobre duración** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:377`)*:

> "To establish that they are or have been a victim of trafficking and have been compelled to perform the labor or services, applicants do not need to show that they were victimized for a defined length of time. For example, in the context of involuntary servitude, the duration of time can be of varying length and may be short in duration.[33]"

*(Footnote [33], `part-b-chapter-2-eligibility-requirements.md:618`: "18 U.S.C. 1584 requires that involuntary servitude be for 'any term,' which suggests that the duration of time can be short.")*

**Sobre alternancia voluntario/involuntario** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:381`)*:

> "The labor or services at issue also do not have to be coerced in every instance. There could be time periods in which the labor or services were voluntary and time periods in which they were involuntary. However, the labor or services must have been involuntary for at least some portion of the time that the applicant was performing the work to constitute labor trafficking."

**Sobre propósito del traficante** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:383`)*:

> "There is no requirement regarding the length of time the labor or services are performed or the means used to induce the acts. Nevertheless, the applicant must establish that the trafficker acted for the purpose of subjecting the victim to involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage, slavery, or a commercial sex act. The duration of the labor or services may be relevant in assessing the trafficker's purpose or motivation."

**Sobre intent del perpetrador** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:387`)*:

> "An applicant must provide facts and evidence establishing the perpetrator's intent or purpose to demonstrate that trafficking has occurred. To establish that they are a victim of a severe form of trafficking in persons, an applicant must demonstrate that the perpetrator engaged in a specific prohibited action through the use of force, fraud, or coercion for the purpose of inducing a commercial sex act or subjecting the applicant to involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage, or slavery."

**Sobre consentimiento previo y forced labor** *(`part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:393`)*:

> "A person may voluntarily consent to be smuggled. In contrast, an act of trafficking must involve both a particular means, such as the use of force, fraud, or coercion, and a particular purpose, such as subjection to involuntary servitude or a commercial sex act. Federal law prohibits forced labor regardless of the victim's initial consent to work.[35]"

## Debt Bondage

### Definición legal

**Estatuto — 22 U.S.C. § 7102(7)** *(fuente: `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:116-122`)*:

> "The term 'debt bondage' means the status or condition of a debtor arising from a pledge by the debtor of his or her personal services or of those of a person under his or her control as a security for debt, if the value of those services as reasonably assessed is not applied toward the liquidation of the debt or the length and nature of those services are not respectively limited and defined."

**Regulación — 8 CFR § 214.201** *(fuente: `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:37`)*:

> "*Debt bondage* means the status or condition of a debtor arising from a pledge by the debtor of their personal services or those of a person under their control as a security for debt, if the value of those services as reasonably assessed is not applied toward the liquidation of the debt or the length and nature of those services are not respectively limited and defined."

**Policy Manual — 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 2, §B.4** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:343`)*:

> "'Debt bondage' is the status or condition of a debtor arising from a pledge by the debtor of the personal services of the debtor or those of a person under the debtor's control as a security for debt, if the value of those services as reasonably assessed is not applied toward the liquidation of the debt or the length and nature of those services are not limited and defined.[28] Appendix: Case Law References for T Visa Adjudications [3 USCIS-PM B, Appendices Tab] lists decisions that may provide additional clarification on the concept of debt bondage."

### Observaciones USCIS

- El PM línea 219-221 cita la definición y remite al apéndice de case law. No contiene sub-secciones adicionales, listas no-exhaustivas, ni elaboración operativa.
- En `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:387`, debt bondage aparece junto a involuntary servitude, peonage y slavery como uno de los purposes que el aplicante debe demostrar.

### Elementos recurrentes

- **"status or condition of a debtor"** — frase nuclear idéntica en las tres fuentes.
- **"pledge … of personal services … as a security for debt"** — estructura presente en las tres fuentes.
- **Dos condiciones disyuntivas en la definición estatutaria.** El estatuto las separa con "or": (a) "the value of those services as reasonably assessed is not applied toward the liquidation of the debt"; (b) "the length and nature of those services are not respectively limited and defined".
- **"reasonably assessed"** — frase literal del estatuto, regulación y PM.
- **Variantes léxicas observables entre estatuto y regulación:** "his or her" (estatuto) vs. "their" (regulación).

## Peonage

### Autoridad

| Nivel | Fuente | Cita exacta del locator |
|---|---|---|
| Estatuto TVPA | (sin entrada autónoma — usado en § 7102(11)(B)) | `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:186` |
| Regulación | 8 CFR § 214.201 | `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:63` |
| Policy Manual | 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 2, §B.4 — "Peonage" | `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:332-340` |

### Definición legal

**Estatuto:** el TVPA 22 U.S.C. § 7102 no contiene entrada autónoma de "peonage". El término aparece como uno de los fines del trafficking en § 7102(11)(B).

**Regulación — 8 CFR § 214.201** *(fuente: `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:63`)*:

> "*Peonage* means a status or condition of involuntary servitude based upon real or alleged indebtedness."

**Policy Manual — 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 2, §B.4** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:334`)*:

> "Peonage is a status or condition of involuntary servitude based upon real or alleged indebtedness.[27] In other words, the perpetrator compels the victim into involuntary servitude in order to satisfy a real or artificially-created debt."

### Observaciones USCIS

**Sobre deuda contractual voluntaria** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:337`)*:

> "When a debtor voluntarily enters into a contract to pay off a debt, that debtor, like any other contractor, can choose at any time to break the contract. It does not matter that a debtor entered into a contract agreeing to perform services or labor for the creditor. There is a clear distinction between peonage and voluntarily agreeing to perform labor or provide services in payment of a debt."

**Sobre relación de peonage con involuntary servitude** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:339`)*:

> "In determining whether an applicant has been a victim of peonage, officers should follow the guidance above related to involuntary servitude, with the added element that the victim was held in involuntary servitude in order to satisfy a real or artificially-created debt. If the facts support a finding that the applicant was a victim of peonage, USCIS officers should also consider the applicant a victim of involuntary servitude."

### Elementos recurrentes

- **Estructura peonage / involuntary servitude.** En lugar de paráfrasis del preparador, la relación está declarada literalmente por el PM línea 217 (cita arriba): "officers should follow the guidance above related to involuntary servitude, with the added element that the victim was held in involuntary servitude in order to satisfy a real or artificially-created debt."
- **Direccionalidad declarada en el PM línea 217:** "If the facts support a finding that the applicant was a victim of peonage, USCIS officers should also consider the applicant a victim of involuntary servitude."
- **Variantes léxicas observables.** Regulación: "real or alleged indebtedness". PM: "real or artificially-created debt".
- **Distinción explícita con contrato voluntario** — frase literal del PM línea 215 (cita arriba).
- **Ausencia de entrada estatutaria autónoma.** La definición autónoma aparece en la regulación y en el PM, no en 22 U.S.C. § 7102.

## Commercial Sex Act

### Autoridad

| Nivel | Fuente | Cita exacta del locator |
|---|---|---|
| Estatuto TVPA | 22 U.S.C. § 7102(4) | `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:66-69` |
| Regulación | 8 CFR § 214.201 | `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:35` |
| Policy Manual | 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 2, §B.5 — "Definition of Sex Trafficking" | `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:349-352` |
| Regulación (sex trafficking) | 8 CFR § 214.201 | `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:73` |
| Estatuto (sex trafficking) | 22 U.S.C. § 7102(12) | `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:189-193` |

### Definición legal

**Estatuto — 22 U.S.C. § 7102(4)** *(fuente: `22-usc-7102-definitions.md:66-69`)*:

> "The term 'commercial sex act' means any sex act on account of which anything of value is given to or received by any person."

**Regulación — 8 CFR § 214.201** *(fuente: `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:35`)*:

> "*Commercial sex act* means any sex act on account of which anything of value is given to or received by any person."

**Policy Manual — 3 USCIS-PM B, Ch. 2, §B.5** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:351`)*:

> "Sex trafficking is the recruitment, harboring, transportation, provision, obtaining, patronizing, or soliciting of a person for the purpose of a commercial sex act.[29] A commercial sex act is any sex act on account of which anything of value is given to or received by any person.[30] For sex trafficking to constitute a severe form of trafficking in persons, the commercial sex act must generally have been induced by force, fraud, or coercion.[31] However, a minor under the age of 18 who engages in a commercial sex act meets the definition of a victim of a severe form of trafficking in persons without having to show force, fraud, or coercion. This is because minors under the age of 18 cannot consent to sexual acts."

### Observaciones USCIS

**Sobre menores y la excepción a "means"** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:351`)*:

> "However, a minor under the age of 18 who engages in a commercial sex act meets the definition of a victim of a severe form of trafficking in persons without having to show force, fraud, or coercion. This is because minors under the age of 18 cannot consent to sexual acts."

*(Cita cruzada en regulación, `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:279`)*:

> "Except in instances of sex trafficking involving victims under 18 years of age, severe forms of trafficking in persons must involve both a particular means (force, fraud, or coercion) and a particular end or a particular intended end (sex trafficking, involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage, or slavery)"

**Sobre el "purpose-only" path** *(fuente: `8-cfr-214-subpart-c-t-nonimmigrant-status.md:281`)*:

> "(2) If an applicant has not performed labor or services, or a commercial sex act, they must establish that they were recruited, transported, harbored, provided, or obtained for the purposes of subjection to sex trafficking, involuntary servitude, peonage, debt bondage, or slavery, or patronized or solicited for the purposes of subjection to sex trafficking."

**Sobre compensación no determinativa** *(fuente: `part-b-victims-of-trafficking.md:361`)*:

> "A worker who is paid some or all the promised wages may still be a victim of trafficking. The fact that an applicant was paid a salary or wage for work is not determinative of whether the applicant was subjected to one of the federal trafficking crimes."

### Elementos recurrentes

- **"any sex act on account of which anything of value is given to or received by any person"** — frase nuclear idéntica en estatuto, regulación y PM.
- **"anything of value"** — frase literal sin cuantificación numérica en ninguna fuente del canon T-Visa.
- **"given to or received by any person"** — frase literal: la fuente abarca tanto a la víctima como a cualquier tercero como receptor del valor.
- **Excepción de menores como bright line.** Para víctimas <18 años en sex trafficking, las tres fuentes (estatuto § 7102(11)(A); regulación 8 CFR § 214.206(b)(1) — línea 303; PM línea 229) declaran que NO se requiere force/fraud/coercion.
- **Justificación de la excepción en el PM:** "minors under the age of 18 cannot consent to sexual acts" — cita literal del PM línea 229.
- **"purpose-only" path con verbos exclusivos en sex trafficking** — el regulador 8 CFR § 214.206(b)(2) lista "patronized or solicited for the purposes of subjection to sex trafficking" como acción suficiente sin consumación.
- **Compensación no excluye trafficking** — frase literal del PM línea 239.
- **Sex trafficking y commercial sex act como entradas separadas en el estatuto:** § 7102(4) define "commercial sex act"; § 7102(12) define "sex trafficking".

# Hipótesis preliminares del preparador (NO extracción — sujetas a corrección del abogado)

> Esta sección contiene observaciones interpretativas del preparador no-abogado. **No son extracción**, no están respaldadas literalmente por las fuentes, y pueden ser incorrectas. El abogado las puede corregir, eliminar o adoptar; el resto del documento NO depende de ellas. Quedan aquí separadas visualmente para que sea claro qué es fuente y qué es conjetura mía.

## H1 — Sobre Recruiting como acto inicial del esquema

Recruiting es el primer verbo en la cadena tanto en § 7102(11)(B) (labor trafficking) como en § 7102(12) (sex trafficking). Esto sugiere — y solo sugiere — un orden cronológico implícito (recruiting precede a los otros actos). Las fuentes NO declaran este orden. Es posible que el orden léxico sea convencional y no temporal.

## H2 — Sobre el peso operativo en Coercion frente a Force y Fraud

Coercion tiene definición estatutaria (§ 7102(3)), regulatoria (8 CFR § 214.201) y un capítulo PM extenso (líneas 73-164 con cuatro modalidades sub-divididas). Force y Fraud no tienen contraparte equivalente: aparecen exclusivamente como términos en la fórmula "force, fraud, or coercion" sin ser definidos. El PM clasifica ejemplos de violencia física bajo "physical coercion" (no bajo "force") y ejemplos de promesas falsas bajo "scheme/plan/pattern" (no bajo "fraud" autónomo).

Esta asimetría de tratamiento es un hecho verificable. **Mi conjetura interpretativa**: que esto sugiere que el peso operativo del análisis de medios recae en coercion. No está dicho por las fuentes. Es plausible que en case law o en práctica de adjudicación se trate fraud o force como ejes autónomos.

## H3 — Sobre el estándar "reasonable person" como híbrido

La frase regulatoria "reasonable person of the same background and in the same circumstances" combina, en mi lectura, dos elementos: un test objetivo ("reasonable person") con una modulación subjetiva ("of the same background and in the same circumstances"). Las fuentes no lo nombran como "estándar híbrido"; eso es vocabulario mío.

## H4 — Sobre la aparición de Patronization en la sección de Involuntary Servitude

El PM línea 198 lista "patronization" entre las acciones que un oficial puede evaluar al analizar involuntary servitude. Sin embargo, las definiciones estatutaria y regulatoria de "patronizing" lo restringen a sex trafficking. Mi lectura es que la aparición de "patronization" en línea 198 no convierte a patronizing en acto válido de labor trafficking — el contexto es la evaluación del propósito del perpetrador, no la calificación del acto. **Pero el abogado debe decidir** si esa lectura es correcta o si la aparición cruzada abre una vía doctrinal distinta.

## H5 — Sobre el umbral de Slavery frente a Involuntary Servitude

La frase "complete and total ownership or control" en la formulación del PM para slavery (línea 223) usa adjetivos absolutos ("complete and total"). La definición de involuntary servitude, en cambio, admite escenarios de control parcial o condicional (por ejemplo, "may occur where a victim initially voluntarily agrees… but is later forced", línea 176). **Mi lectura**: slavery requiere un umbral más alto de control. Las fuentes no lo declaran así explícitamente y el PM mismo dice que slavery "is not defined".

## H6 — Sobre la recursividad de Slavery

La formulación del PM para slavery la define en términos de "for the purpose of subjecting the victim to forced labor or services". El término "forced labor" en el PM remite a involuntary servitude. **Mi lectura**: la formulación del PM para slavery es recursiva — define un fin (slavery) en términos de otro fin (forced labor / involuntary servitude). Esto no está declarado por la fuente como recursividad y puede ser ruido del lenguaje natural más que estructura doctrinal.

## H7 — Sobre la pregunta de cierre de ausencias de definición

El canon T-Visa cita repetidamente estatutos criminales federales (18 U.S.C. §§ 1584, 1589) en footnotes. Posibles vías doctrinales para cerrar las ausencias incluyen importarlos al canon T-Visa, tratarlas como fronteras doctrinales abiertas, o seguir la práctica del PM (uso sin delimitación). **Decisión del abogado.** No la cierro aquí.
